About Procurement Archaeology

An independent research publication that reconstructs how public projects are bought, who profits, where accountability breaks down, and what the documents actually prove.

AB
Abdulrehman Bhatti

Founder & Lead Researcher. Researcher and writer covering public procurement, supply chains, audit findings, and the evidence behind public spending.

Procurement Archaeology exists because the most important part of a public project is often the part that is hardest to see: the contract. A hospital, a border system, a rail line, or a cloud platform begins as a political promise. It becomes a set of tender documents, framework agreements, subcontracts, amendments, and payment records. Those records rarely tell a clean story. They are spread across portals, buried in PDFs, written in different languages, and updated at different speeds.

My work starts with a simple discipline: follow the document, not the press release. I read award notices, contract amendments, audit reports, court filings, corporate registrations, and parliamentary questions. I compare what was announced with what was signed, and what was signed with what was paid. When the record is incomplete, I say so. When a number cannot be verified, I do not repeat it as if it were settled.

What this publication does

Each investigation begins with a procurement question that can be tested against evidence. Who awarded the contract? Which legal instrument was used? Was the competition open, negotiated, restricted, or a framework call-off? Did the scope change? Who were the subcontractors? What did the auditor, regulator, or court find? The answers are assembled into a narrative that a reader can check, not a verdict that a reader has to trust.

The interactive tools on the home page are deliberately separate from the editorial record. They are a demonstration of how structured procurement data can be explored: search, a supply-chain graph, a project map, and a source archive. The demo contract table is synthetic and clearly labelled. The investigations are different. They cite public records and link to the source material.

How data is labelled

Demonstration data means records generated to show how the interface works. It is not a live feed, not a statement about any real company, and not evidence for any claim. It is labelled wherever it appears.

Public-record analysis means an article or page built from official notices, audit reports, court documents, corporate registers, or other verifiable sources. Those sources are listed with links. If a source is unavailable, the article describes the gap rather than inventing a substitute.

Editorial independence and funding

Procurement Archaeology is supported by advertising. Advertising is separated from editorial decisions. Advertisers do not receive advance copies, approval rights, or influence over what is published. The site does not accept payment for a favourable story, a removed finding, or a changed conclusion.

The publication does not take sponsorship from companies that are the subject of an active investigation. If that changes, the relationship will be disclosed on the relevant article and on this page.

Corrections and right of reply

Corrections are welcome and are published with a dated note. Before publishing a negative finding about an identifiable company or individual, I make a reasonable effort to offer a right of reply and to reflect any substantive response. A response does not automatically change a finding, but it is part of the record.

If you believe a figure, date, name, or characterization is wrong, email bhattiabdulrehman430@gmail.com with the article URL and the document you are relying on. I will check it against the cited source and update the article if the record requires it.

What this publication is not

It is not a law firm, an audit institution, or a court. It does not make legal findings. It does not allege criminal conduct without a documented court or regulatory finding. It does not treat a risk indicator as proof. It does not publish leaked personal data or material that would identify a private individual without a clear public-interest justification.

Contact

General enquiries, corrections, and research tips: bhattiabdulrehman430@gmail.com. For data or methodology questions, start with the methodology page.

Transparency note: the interactive application is a static demonstration. It does not currently ingest live data from TED, SAM.gov, or Contracts Finder. The editorial articles use public records and link to the official portals where those records live.
